About This Collection

Almost all historians, whatever their religious views, agree that Jesus of Nazareth existed. Most also accept that he was baptized by John the Baptist and crucified under the Roman prefect Pontius Pilate. This site gathers the main ancient evidence behind that agreement.

It also covers famous relics such as the Shroud of Turin, the search for the Ark of the Covenant, and major finds from the Hebrew Bible period. Each item is labeled honestly. Some are widely accepted, some are debated, and some are traditions or claims that have never been verified.

A note on scope: historical evidence can confirm people, places, and events. It cannot settle faith claims such as the resurrection or Jesus' divinity, so those questions are left to theology and personal conviction.

How to read the labels

  • Widely accepted Most scholars accept it as authentic or reliable, whatever their beliefs.
  • Debated Serious scholars disagree about its authenticity, date, or meaning.
  • Context It doesn't mention Jesus directly but shows the world the Bible describes.

Further Reading

These books come from authors across the spectrum, from skeptical to devout:

  • Bart D. Ehrman, Did Jesus Exist? The Historical Argument for Jesus of Nazareth (2012)
  • John P. Meier, A Marginal Jew: Rethinking the Historical Jesus (multi-volume)
  • E. P. Sanders, The Historical Figure of Jesus (1993)
  • Robert E. Van Voorst, Jesus Outside the New Testament (2000)
  • Craig A. Evans, Jesus and His World: The Archaeological Evidence (2012)
  • Richard Bauckham, Jesus and the Eyewitnesses (2006; 2nd ed. 2017)
